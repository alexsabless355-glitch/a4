1. The Evolution of Bloom Taxonomy: 1956 to Anderson and Krathwohl 2001

In 1956, American educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom published the foundational *Taxonomy of Educational Objectives*, establishing a hierarchical framework for categorizing cognitive complexity.

In 2001, Lorin Anderson (a former student of Bloom) and David Krathwohl published a pivotal revision:

They transformed Bloom static nouns into active verbs and reordered the top tiers, reflecting the modern cognitive understanding that **Creating** represents the pinnacle of intellectual synthesis.

2. The Six Cognitive Process Dimensions Explained

The revised taxonomy outlines six ascending cognitive tiers:

  1. Remembering: Retrieving relevant knowledge from long-term memory (recognizing, recalling facts).
  2. Understanding: Constructing meaning from instructional messages (interpreting, exemplifying, classifying, summarizing).
  3. Applying: Executing or implementing a procedure in a given situation.
  4. Analyzing: Breaking material into constituent parts and determining how parts relate to an overall structure (differentiating, organizing, attributing).
  5. Evaluating: Making judgments based on criteria and standards (checking, critiquing proofs).
  6. Creating: Reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure (generating hypotheses, designing solutions, producing original models).

3. The Four Knowledge Dimensions: Factual, Conceptual, Procedural, Metacognitive

Anderson and Krathwohl introduced a two-dimensional matrix crossing the six cognitive processes against four knowledge categories:

  • Factual Knowledge: Terminology and specific details.
  • Conceptual Knowledge: Classifications, principles, generalizations, and interrelationships.
  • Procedural Knowledge: Subject-specific skills, algorithms, techniques, and criteria for determining when to use them.
  • Metacognitive Knowledge: Strategic knowledge, awareness of cognitive tasks, and self-knowledge.
